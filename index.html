Getting Started with Hiking

New to hiking? Here's everything you need to know to start your journey.

First Steps

  • Start with easy, well-marked trails
  • Hike with a friend or group initially
  • Check the weather forecast before heading out
  • Tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back
  • Start early to avoid afternoon thunderstorms

Plan Your First Hike

  1. Research local trails suitable for beginners
  2. Check trail conditions and closures
  3. Prepare your gear the night before
  4. Pack plenty of water and snacks
  5. Wear appropriate clothing and footwear

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Don't overestimate your fitness level
  • Avoid hiking alone as a beginner
  • Don't forget to check the weather
  • Never leave trash on the trail
  • Don't ignore signs of fatigue or dehydration

Gear Guide

Essential equipment for safe and enjoyable hiking.

Footwear

Good hiking boots or shoes are crucial. Look for:

  • Proper fit with wiggle room for toes
  • Good ankle support for uneven terrain
  • Waterproof or water-resistant materials
  • Aggressive tread for traction

Clothing

Dress in layers for changing conditions:

  • Moisture-wicking base layer
  • Insulating mid-layer (fleece or down)
  • Waterproof/windproof outer shell
  • Extra socks (wool or synthetic)

Essentials to Pack

Water

At least 2 liters per person

Food

Snacks and lunch for energy

Navigation

Map, compass, or GPS device

Sun Protection

Sunscreen, hat, sunglasses

First Aid

Basic kit with essentials

Light

Headlamp or flashlight

Budget Tips

You don't need to spend a fortune to start hiking:

  • Borrow gear from friends initially
  • Check thrift stores for outdoor clothing
  • Look for sales at outdoor retailers
  • Invest in good footwear first

Safety Tips

Stay safe and enjoy the outdoors responsibly.

The 10 Essentials

Always carry these items on every hike:

  1. Navigation (map and compass)
  2. Sun protection (sunglasses and sunscreen)
  3. Insulation (extra clothing)
  4. Illumination (headlamp/flashlight)
  5. First-aid supplies
  6. Fire (matches/lighter)
  7. Repair kit and tools
  8. Nutrition (extra food)
  9. Hydration (extra water)
  10. Emergency shelter

Weather Awareness

  • Check forecast before leaving
  • Know the signs of approaching storms
  • Descend from peaks if thunderstorms approach
  • Be prepared for temperature drops at higher elevations

Wildlife Safety

  • Make noise to avoid surprising animals
  • Store food properly (bear canisters where required)
  • Keep a safe distance from all wildlife
  • Know what animals live in the area you're hiking

Emergency Preparedness

  • Carry a fully charged phone (but don't rely on it)
  • Know your location using maps or GPS
  • Pack a whistle to signal for help
  • Learn basic first aid and CPR

Hiking 101

Learn the fundamentals of hiking etiquette, techniques, and terminology.

Trail Etiquette

  • Yield to uphill hikers - Step aside and give space
  • Stay on the trail - Protect vegetation and prevent erosion
  • Pack it in, pack it out - Leave no trace
  • Right of way - Hikers going uphill have priority
  • Leash your dog - Where required, keep pets under control

Hiking Techniques

  • Pace yourself - Start slow and maintain a steady rhythm
  • Use trekking poles - Reduce knee strain on descents
  • Step consciously - Watch your footing on technical terrain
  • Rest regularly - Take breaks to hydrate and snack

Common Terminology

Blaze
Paint marks on trees or posts marking the trail
Switchback
Zigzag path used to climb steep slopes
Cairn
Stack of rocks marking the trail, especially above treeline
Tree line
Elevation above which trees cannot grow
Base weight
Weight of your pack without consumables (water, food)

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from new hikers answered.

How fit do I need to be to start hiking?

You don't need to be an athlete! Start with short, easy trails and gradually increase difficulty. Regular walking is good preparation.

What should I eat before a hike?

Eat a balanced meal with carbs and protein 1-2 hours before hiking. Avoid heavy, greasy foods that might cause stomach upset.

How much water should I bring?

A general rule is 0.5 liters per hour of hiking in moderate conditions. Bring more in hot weather or on strenuous trails.

Can I hike if it's raining?

Yes, with proper gear! Wear waterproof clothing and avoid exposed ridges during thunderstorms. Check trail conditions as some trails become hazardous when wet.

Should I hike alone?

Beginners should hike with others. As you gain experience, solo hiking can be rewarding but requires extra safety precautions and telling someone your plans.

What do I do if I get lost?

STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan. Stay calm, don't panic. Use your map and compass, retrace your steps if possible, and signal for help if needed.
